Chapter 3

Evaluation Methodology

Open-ended AI is hard to grade: there's no single right answer, and the tasks are often ones humans find hard to judge too. This chapter is the toolbox — the ways to score model output, from cheap exact matching to using an AI model as the judge.

The evaluation toolbox

Five ways to score an output, arranged from cheap-and-automatic to rich-and-expensive. Real systems mix several.

AI as a judge — and its blind spots

The most flexible evaluation method is also a model, which means it has failure modes of its own.

Perplexity — the model's surprise meter

One metric comes from the model itself: how surprised it is by a piece of text.

Key takeaways

  • Prefer verifiable tasks If an output can be checked automatically — tests pass, answer matches — evaluation gets 10× easier. Design for checkability.
  • Similarity needs references Lexical and semantic similarity only work when you have good reference answers — and word overlap can mislead.
  • AI judges scale, with care They make evaluation continuous and cheap, but carry position, verbosity, and self-preference biases. Calibrate against humans.
  • Perplexity ≠ quality It measures how predictable text is to a model — useful for filtering and contamination checks, not for grading answers.
